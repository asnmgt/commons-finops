# About Commons FinOps

Commons FinOps is a documented financial and operations practice for fiscally sponsored, polycentric, commons-oriented institutions. It translates Elinor Ostrom's eight design principles for enduring commons into the operational requirements that fiscal sponsors, foundations, and OSS funding cores must meet to function.

The practice sits at the seam between three rooms that most practitioners treat separately: nonprofit fiscal sponsorship, open source software funding, and digital governance research. Each room has its own vocabulary, its own funders, its own regulatory landscape. Commons FinOps is what the three rooms look like when you read them from the operator's seat: the books, the policies, the audit trails, the intake gates, the monitoring cadence, the conflict resolution paths, the graduated sanctions.

## Where the practice comes from

The practice was developed and is maintained by Andrew Ngeseyan, drawing on 18+ years of senior finance and operations leadership across three institutions:

- **Community Roots Charter Schools** (2010-2022 full-time, 2022-present consulting). Twelve years as Chief Financial Officer. $27M+ in lifetime business activity managed across 16 clean federal and state audits, including Title I, IDEA, Single Audit Act, New York State Comptroller, and philanthropic compliance. The foundational chops: restricted fund discipline, audit readiness as a constant frame, multi-year scenario modeling, complex grant accounting, board governance through financial crisis.

- **The LeadersTrust** (2022-2024). Chief Operations and Finance Officer. Built and ran a $27M social impact nonprofit operation from a scrappy start-up, including the financial and operational infrastructure required to host and steward fiscally sponsored programs across a diverse portfolio. Where the fiscal sponsorship chops, the multi-stakeholder reporting muscle, and the audit-ready systems took their current shape.

- **Metagov Inc.** (2025-present). Finance and Operations Director. A 501(c)(3) fiscal sponsor for digital governance research, channeling more than $2M annually across projects in AI safety (Atlas Computing, Public AI), civic tech (Civic AI Tools), deliberative democracy (Talk to the City, Harmonica), and conscious organizational closure (The Wind Down). The live laboratory in which Commons FinOps is being tested, refined, and documented in real time. EisnerAmper serves as the CPA preparing the books for the organization's first independent audit. Karl Mill at Mill Law Center serves as outside counsel.

The practice is offered through ASN Management LLC as a fractional FinOps consultancy for fiscal sponsors, foundations, and OSS funding cores.

## Why it is offered open

Most operational knowledge in fiscal sponsorship is locked inside individual organizations. A new sponsor stands up, reinvents the chart of accounts, builds the intake form from scratch, learns the restricted fund rules the hard way, and produces audit findings that another sponsor solved five years earlier. The cost of that reinvention is borne by the projects that depended on the sponsor functioning sooner.

Commons FinOps is offered open because the operational layer should compound across practitioners, not stay locked to any one of them. The documentation lives under CC BY 4.0 (attribution required, derivative work welcome). Any code or templates live under MIT. Other fiscal sponsors, foundations, and OSS funding cores are invited to fork, adapt, contribute back, and stand up their own implementations without paying the reinvention tax.

The repository, the working group, the methodology, and the public artifacts are the commons. Consulting engagements through ASN Management LLC are the practice. Both are necessary. Neither replaces the other.
