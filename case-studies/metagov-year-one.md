# Metagov Finance and Operations: Historical Context

**Purpose:** A factual record of the finance and operations work performed for Metagov, intended as grounding context for an AI research tool (Perplexity or similar) and as an internal reference. This document describes what was actually done, the decisions reached, and the principles those decisions established. Where figures were still being verified at the time of the work, that status is noted.

**Organization:** Metagov Inc., a 501(c)(3) nonprofit fiscal sponsor and research infrastructure organization focused on digital governance, civic technology, and democratic innovation.

**Role:** Director of Finance and Operations, serving as FinOps Lead.

**Period covered:** Approximately 2025 through mid-2026.

---

## 1. The Engagement at a Glance

The work spanned the full finance and operations function of a fiscal sponsor: onboarding and managing sponsored projects, maintaining the financial system of record, managing restricted grants, running treasury including digital assets, handling compliance, supporting the board, and developing a long-term sustainability plan. Over the period, the portfolio channeled more than $2M in active research funding across projects including Atlas Computing, Public AI, Calm Technology, and the Grant Innovation Lab, with clean reporting trails to funders and project-level accounting.

The organization works closely with Liz Barry as Executive Director and a board including Nathan Schneider, Michael Zargham, and Primavera de Filippi. External audit runs through EisnerAmper. Legal review runs through Karl Mill at Mill Law Center. Metagov's anchor funder for core operations is One Project, with that grant concluding in 2028.

## 2. Fiscal Sponsorship Operations and the Onboarding System

The central operational achievement was moving fiscal sponsorship from ad hoc handling to a documented operating system. This included standard budget templates, project-level pages, bank-rule routing for virtual cards, and approval flows that matured over time from Executive Director approved toward project lead approved.

The institutional knowledge was captured in a Fiscal Sponsorship Onboarding Manual covering the full lifecycle: the fit conversation, the agreement, project setup on the transaction platform, funds intake, spending, communications, compliance, and clean handoffs when projects move on. This was the first time the organization had a written, project lead facing fiscal sponsorship guide.

Intake and pipeline work over the period included reviewing new hosting applications (among them Star Lynx and Democracy Collective, with a default host fee question to confirm on one of them), an Open Machine inquiry where Liz connected Ven Gist and Exeunt with FinOps as the point of contact, and Camille Acey's conscious-closures practice as a mission-aligned prospective project. A recurring principle held throughout: urgency framing from prospective projects should not be allowed to compress the diligence process, since the rushed intake is where inherited problems get missed.

## 3. The Atlas Computing Transition

The Atlas Computing transition to Renaissance Philanthropy (formerly referenced as Convergent Research) was the first major project transition Metagov ran end to end, and it became the template for future transitions.

The financial close-out centered on a transfer in the range of $68K to $68,750, sized deliberately against confirmed amounts rather than projected ones. The work included coordinating a multi-party signing process with Liz, Evan Miyazono, and others, against a hard external deadline of May 31, with legal documents cleared through counsel.

A detailed agreement review surfaced several issues in the executed transfer document that needed correction with counsel before the deadline. The executed PDF differed materially from the original Convergent-drafted template: it named Metagov and Evan Miyazono as co-transferors, listed assets inline rather than on a schedule, and added new representations to Section 5. The review flagged three specific problems: Metagov's signature block incorrectly named it as an LLC rather than a nonprofit corporation as defined in the body of the agreement, the transferee signature header and the body disagreed on Inc. versus LLC, and Evan Miyazono's individual transferor signature line was left blank despite his being named a co-transferor. These were raised with counsel as items needing either a correcting amendment or a re-executed signature page. The episode established a precedent that now informs every agreement: confirm entity naming and signature completeness before execution, because errors in a signed document are far harder to unwind.

Earlier in the relationship, the work also included a formal letter confirming Evan Miyazono's role as CEO of Atlas Computing and explaining the fiscal sponsorship structure, prepared in response to a Silicon Valley Community Foundation grant validation request.

## 4. The AWS Gift Reconciliation and the RenPhil Transfer

A significant analytical engagement involved an Amazon Web Services charitable gift supporting CSLib work by researcher Alexandre Rademaker, who was fiscally sponsored under Atlas Computing and transitioning to Renaissance Philanthropy.

The reconciliation resolved several factual questions by working directly against Open Collective transaction exports and QuickBooks Statement of Activity files. The findings: the gift was structured as two separate annual purchase orders of roughly $75K each, not a single $150K grant in two tranches. Only the first $75K had been received. Three platform entries that appeared to show $225K reconciled to the same single $75K receipt, because two same-day entries were internal Mercury-to-platform reconciliation postings rather than new inflows. The only expenditure confidently attributable to the grant was a single invoice billing at the pro-rata grant rate and explicitly referencing CSLib.

The decisions that followed: the transfer to Renaissance Philanthropy should be sized against the $75K actually on hand, not the full pledged amount; Year 2 funding should ideally be redirected by Amazon directly to Renaissance Philanthropy rather than routed through Metagov again; and the fiscal sponsorship fee question had to be resolved before locking the transfer amount. This episode is the clearest demonstration of two durable principles: the general ledger is authoritative and the transaction platform is only a subset of activity, and commitments are sized against confirmed receipts only, never against pledges.

## 5. Financial Systems and Reconciliation Discipline

QuickBooks Online served as the authoritative financial system, with Open Collective as the project-level transaction platform, Mercury and Axos for banking, Wise for international transfers, Bitwave for digital asset management, Deel for contractor payroll, and a PEO for W-2 payroll. SaasAnt supported bulk QuickBooks updates, and a Bill.com accounts payable automation was in progress.

The reconciliation discipline that recurred throughout the work treated the general ledger as the single source of truth. Open Collective exports were understood to omit wire transfers, ACH receipts, and bank-direct payments, and to include internal transfer postings that are not new inflows. When figures failed to reconcile, the resolution path ran through QuickBooks, not through platform transaction tabs. Source verification was done directly against QuickBooks Excel exports and Open Collective CSVs using scripting tools, and verified figures were always established before any funder-facing language was drafted.

## 6. Treasury and Digital Assets

Treasury work included standing up reliable processes for non-traditional asset intake. The posture was conservative and stablecoin-weighted, appropriate for funds largely held on behalf of projects. The digital asset workflow ran from on-chain receipt into an institutional multi-signature wallet, through Coinbase Prime as the off-ramp intermediary for converting USDC to fiat, and into Mercury, since the bank cannot accept direct on-chain deposits. Routine treasury activity over the period included monitoring USDC and ETH movements, processing weekly payment runs that consistently settled within two to three business days, and managing virtual cards for project-level subscriptions. Crypto audit-trail documentation was an active workstream in support of the first independent audit.

## 7. Grant Management

Active grant relationships over the period included Survival and Flourishing Fund, the Ethereum Foundation, Mozilla, Earth Convergence Group, Berggruen Institute, One Project, and Amazon Web Services, along with project-specific awards. Representative grant operations included a $100K wire from Mercury to Wise with confirmed receipt, an Atlas-related ARIA grant of £60,000 tracked against Open Collective reflection, a One Project disbursement of $150K, a Claneil Foundation grant of $3,500 supporting a wind-down practice with the corresponding payment to Camille Acey processed, and a Protopian prize of $10K.

A persistent SVCF issue involved a PaymentWorks tax ID mismatch and ACH validation, which was resolved by pivoting to wire payment. Throughout, two grant principles held: when a funder asks for available funding they generally mean unrestricted reserves rather than total cash, since most of Metagov's cash is restricted pass-through; and funder concentration should be expressed against core operations rather than total organizational spend to avoid distorting the picture.

## 8. The Endowment Proposal

A major strategic deliverable was the Metagov Research Endowment proposal, developed for the board and refined across several passes. The target is a $1M endowment that funds the durable infrastructure of the organization rather than individual projects: Research Directors, collaborative grant-writing support, community tools, data infrastructure, and administrative systems.

The framing was deliberately vision-driven rather than survival-driven. The distinction was made explicit: replacing the One Project anchor funder is a survival pitch, while capitalizing the only integrated research infrastructure for digital governance is a vision pitch, and donors respond to vision. The Open Source Endowment at endowment.dev served as the peer model and a potential fundraising venue, having raised $755K from 109 donors at a 5% spend rate, with the UN Open Source Week funder gathering on June 22, 2026 identified as a natural first venue.

A key narrative upgrade came from Seth Frey's reframe: Metagov should take institutional credit for the grant funding its Research Directors have brought in through their host universities, shifting the organization's identity from linker and connector to manager and orchestrator. Seth's own confirmed figures were substantial, exceeding $5.5M including a single $3.4M anchor grant. A discipline was applied here that matters: only Seth's figures were confirmed, so other Research Director numbers were described generally pending a survey rather than asserted. A separate vague claim of $10M+ in advisory relationships, carried over from an early draft, was flagged as unverifiable as written, traced to its source in the Executive Director's November 2025 board report, and recommended for replacement with concrete verified figures. The proposal also reframed Atlas Computing's departure as a graduation story rather than a loss, evidence that the full project lifecycle works as intended.

## 9. Board and Governance Support

Board support included preparing talking points and meeting guides for board meetings, organized around the items owned in finance and operations: the fiscal sponsorship policy vote, the endowment co-presentation, and conflict-of-interest bylaws closeout. These materials included per-item talking points, anticipated question-and-answer blocks, carryover task tables, back-pocket sourcing material, and explicit lists of topics not to surface, such as unannounced project transitions and unverified figures. The governing communication principle was to surface structural realities rather than obscure them, on the understanding that boards respond better to honest deficit framing paired with a mitigation strategy than to a sanitized narrative.

## 10. Operating Cadence and Infrastructure

The operational rhythm included weekly all-staff Slack standups produced in two variants, a story-driven version giving narrative context and a concise version for quick scanning, along with regular FinOps check-ins with Liz. To replace a scattered system of a running Slack agenda, inbox-as-tracker, and mental tracking, a Coda task-tracking system was designed and built out: six tables with separate views for Andrew and Liz, weekly rituals, and a Friday priority push. A communications automation strategy mapped the integrated Superhuman, Otter, Slack, and Coda stack with supporting rituals, developed as a direct response to feedback on operational pace. The agreed method for sharing Coda infrastructure into AI workflows was per-conversation CSV or PDF export.

## 11. Compliance

Compliance obligations active over the period included Form 990 preparation, a Massachusetts independent audit triggered by crossing the state audit threshold (Metagov's first independent audit, conducted by EisnerAmper), and 1099 issuance. A specific 1099 issue arose around travel and reimbursements being potentially included incorrectly in contractor compensation, prompting follow-up with the CPAs to confirm and refile if needed. A W-2 discrepancy requiring escalation was also flagged. The first audit in particular represented a significant operational lift, and the relationships with the external auditor and outside counsel were maintained as standing rather than assembled under deadline pressure.

## 12. The Year-One Accomplishments, Summarized

By the May 2026 annual review, the year-one record included: more than $2M in fiscal sponsorship funding channeled with clean reporting trails; the Atlas to Renaissance Philanthropy transition delivered within deadline with counsel-cleared documents and an approved transfer letter, establishing the template for future transitions; the first written Fiscal Sponsorship Onboarding Manual; the Coda task-tracking system designed and being built; a documented communications automation strategy; reliable treasury and crypto intake processes; and the operationalization of Open Collective from ad hoc use into a documented operating system. The named growth areas were administrative rhythm, email triage architecture, and proactive priority surfacing.
