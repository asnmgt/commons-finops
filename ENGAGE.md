# How to Engage with Commons FinOps

Commons FinOps is offered at three layers: community (open and free), diagnostic (paid, scoped), and embedded (paid, ongoing). The choice depends on where your institution is and what you need next.

If you are unsure which layer fits, open a [GitHub issue](https://github.com/asnmgt/commons-finops/issues) or email andrew@asnmgt.com with a short description of your context. A 20-minute call is usually enough to point you to the right starting place.

---

## Layer 1: Community (open and free)

The repository itself is the first layer. The eight-principles narrative, the Financial Operations Handbook, the Foundation Readiness Guide, the Commons Language Glossary, and the Metagov year-one case study are all under open license. Read them, fork them, adapt them, and use them in your own institution at no cost. Attribution under CC BY 4.0 is appreciated.

Beyond the repo, community engagement happens in three places:

- **GitHub issues and pull requests** on this repository. Corrections, extensions, new templates, case studies from your own institution, translations into adjacent governance frames. See [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](./CONTRIBUTING.md) for the intake process.
- **Working group sessions** (in development). A monthly call modeled on the SciOS Resilient Data Futures working group and similar OSS funding peer rooms. Open to fiscal sponsors, foundation finance and operations leads, OSS funding designers, and digital governance researchers who want to stress-test methodology, share templates, and compare operating contexts. Watch the repo or email to be added when the cadence launches.
- **Conference and workshop participation**. Commons FinOps work has been presented at the UN Open Source Week funder gathering (NYC, June 22, 2026) and submitted to the Institute of Open Science Practices showcase (Leiden, Q3/Q4 2026). If your event is in this neighborhood, reach out.

Cost: free.

## Layer 2: Diagnostic (paid, scoped, time-bounded)

When your institution needs a structured outside read on where you stand, scoped diagnostic engagements deliver a documented assessment and a prioritized action list.

Two of the most common diagnostic shapes:

**Foundation Readiness Diagnostic.** A two-sided assessment for prospective sponsees evaluating their readiness to be hosted, or for sponsors evaluating their readiness to take a project in. Includes a working session, document review, the four-core-intake-risk evaluation, and a written readiness report with red and green flags. Useful before an intake conversation, before a transfer agreement, or before a funder due diligence call. Typical engagement: two to three weeks.

**Audit Readiness Review.** A document and controls assessment in preparation for a first independent audit or a state audit threshold crossing. Includes chart of accounts review, restricted fund accounting check, controls narrative drafting, and a written remediation list ahead of fieldwork. Useful for fiscal sponsors crossing their first audit threshold or organizations whose CPA has flagged structural issues. Typical engagement: three to four weeks.

Cost: fixed-fee diagnostic pricing. Quoted on scope after an intake call.

## Layer 3: Embedded (paid, ongoing fractional engagement)

For institutions that need Commons FinOps as a sustained operational function, fractional embedded engagement through ASN Management LLC takes the diagnostic-and-recommend pattern and converts it into doing-the-work-with-you. Used by organizations that need a finance and operations leader but cannot or should not hire one full-time yet.

Embedded engagement typically includes:

- Standing weekly working session with the Executive Director and finance leadership
- Direct work on the books, the chart of accounts, the intake pipeline, the monitoring cadence, and the audit preparation
- Board-facing financial narrative drafting and presentation support
- Funder due diligence packets, restricted fund tracking, and grant lifecycle management
- Treasury policy and digital asset workflow design where applicable
- Documentation transfer: every artifact created in the engagement becomes the institution's own operating system, not a deliverable that walks out the door when the engagement ends

A typical engagement runs 90 days minimum (a Commons FinOps Charter sprint to stand up the operational dataset) and often extends into ongoing retainer for monitoring, audit support, and board cycle work.

Cost: monthly retainer, quoted on scope.
